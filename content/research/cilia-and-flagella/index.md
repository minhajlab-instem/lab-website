## Overview

Motile and sensory cilia are hair-like organelles that protrude from eukaryotic cells, executing fluid movement, cell propulsion, and sensory signal transduction. At the core of every cilium is the axoneme, built of nine doublet microtubules arranged around a central pair.

## Our Approach

Our lab investigates how doublet microtubules achieve specialized stability and recruitment properties:
1. Identifying inner junction proteins (such as FAP20) that stabilize the B-tubule junction and recruit tubulin to the lattice.
2. Characterizing how tubulin glycylation and glutamylation modulate ciliary motors, intraflagellar transport (IFT), and membrane receptor localization.
3. Unraveling molecular dysfunctions in ciliopathies—genetic disorders affecting respiratory, reproductive, and sensory systems.
