## Overview

Septins constitute a unique, GTP-binding cytoskeletal component found from yeast to humans. Often considered the "fourth component" of the cytoskeleton alongside actin, microtubules, and intermediate filaments, septins form heterooligomeric complexes that assemble into higher-order filaments, rings, and gauze-like networks at the cell cortex and membrane boundaries.

## Our Approach

Our lab combines **X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM, and biochemical reconstitution** to dissect:
1. The structural basis of mammalian septin filament formation at G-interface and NC-interface junctions.
2. The role of GTP binding and hydrolysis in triggering conformational changes that drive filament polymerization and bundling.
3. Septin coordination with actin and microtubule networks during cytokinesis, cell division, and cellular compartmentalization.
