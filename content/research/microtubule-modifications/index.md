## Overview

Microtubules are dynamic cylindrical polymers essential for intracellular transport, cell division, and cellular architecture. The functional diversification of microtubules is governed by the **"Tubulin Code"** — a combination of distinct $\alpha$- and $\beta$-tubulin isotypes and diverse post-translational modifications (PTMs), including:

- **Tyrosination / Detyrosination cycle**: Regulating the initiation and processivity of dynein-dynactin and kinesin motor complexes.
- **Polyglutamylation & Polyglycylation**: Generating complex polymodifications on tubulin C-terminal tails that tune motor velocity, microtubule stability, and ciliary trafficking.

## Our Approach

Using reconstitution biochemistry, cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM), and genetically encoded live-cell biosensors, our lab investigates:
1. How specific PTM combinations recruit or inhibit particular microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs) and molecular motors.
2. Development of novel synthetic binders and live-cell nanobody probes to visualize specific tubulin proteoforms in real time.
3. Disease mechanisms linking tubulin tyrosine ligase (TTL) mutations and perturbations in tubulin tyrosination to cardiomyopathies and neurological disorders.
